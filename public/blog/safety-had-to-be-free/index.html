The story behind VAIRIFY · Part two of four

Safety Had to Be Free

We built the protections first. Then we made a decision that changed the entire business model: safety would never depend on whether a member could afford it.

By Brian Anthony · Founder, VAIRIFYPart two of four

Two years later, I was ready to finish VAIRIFY.

The first thing I realized was how much had changed. Technology had changed. AI had changed. Cybersecurity had changed. Jake had continued developing ChainPass, and he was ready when I was.

One thing had not changed: the community still needed better protection.

Job one was clear:

  • Deliver the maximum protection possible for every encounter.
  • Expand the framework to bring as much of the community’s activity as possible into one app.

The Architecture of Anonymity

Before we could add anything else, Jake and I had to finish the foundation: the V.A.I. itself. Now that ChainPass and VAIRIFY were two separate companies, Jake took the process I had designed two years earlier and incorporated it into the ChainPass side.

Jake explained it simply.

“When your platform sends ChainPass a user, we create a 30-character alphanumeric session key and send the user to our licensed KYC provider. The KYC provider handles the government-issued ID and selfie on its own screens. It verifies that the information on the ID is valid and that the person taking the selfie is the person pictured on that ID.

“That proves your user is real.

“When the verification is complete, the verified photograph from that session comes back to ChainPass. We create a unique seven-character alphanumeric identifier, attach it to that verified photograph, and send the V.A.I. back to VAIRIFY.

“From then on, whenever your platform needs to verify that member, you send us the V.A.I. number together with a new live image. ChainPass compares that image with the verified photograph and confirms whether it is the same person. The entire process takes only a few seconds.”

That was the system I had designed two years earlier.

I asked Jake, “So our anonymity boundaries are still intact?”

“Yes,” he said. “If anything, they’re stronger now.”

Once ChainPass creates the V.A.I., the session key connecting that V.A.I. to the original KYC session is permanently destroyed from the ChainPass side.

Jake walked me through it.

“If ChainPass were compelled to identify one of your members, we couldn’t. We don’t have the member’s identity, and we no longer have the session key that could connect the V.A.I. back to the KYC session. Only Veri retains that key.

“If VAIRIFY were compelled, you still wouldn’t have the identity. You have the V.A.I., but you are not the KYC provider’s client and have no direct relationship with their verification record.

“And if one of our KYC providers were compelled, they would have the identity information from the verification they performed, but they would have no V.A.I. number and no connection to VAIRIFY. To them, it is simply a verification session identified by a session key.”

ChainPass was working with three licensed KYC providers, all based in Europe. Each company held only the information it needed to perform its own part of the process.

No single company held the member’s identity and the V.A.I. that represented them.

That was the anonymity structure I designed in the very beginning.

What ChainPass Delivers

Then I told Jake I wanted to add more.

I wanted a background screening and a law-enforcement declaration behind every V.A.I. on the VAIRIFY platform. I also wanted a way for VAIRIFY to administer binding agreements between verified members and confirm that the member accepting the agreement was actually the verified person behind that V.A.I.

Jake and I took those requirements back to the lawyers. After several conferences, we agreed that ChainPass, as the independent third party providing the verified credential, could administer the screening and law-enforcement declaration and issue a signature agreement allowing VAIRIFY to bind agreements to that V.A.I. with a facial scan.

That gave VAIRIFY exactly what I had been looking for.

A member could remain anonymous inside the platform, while VAIRIFY could still know that the person entering an agreement was a real, verified adult and the same person who had originally received that V.A.I.

What resulted was protection layered directly onto the V.A.I.

Every V.A.I. delivered to VAIRIFY could now arrive with a completed background screening, a law-enforcement declaration, and the signature agreement necessary for VAIRIFY to administer mutual-consent agreements between verified members.

And because ChainPass could authenticate the member again in seconds, those protections could be applied to an actual encounter just as quickly.

That became VAI-CHECK.

VAI-CHECK

Verification was one of the most common issues that came up throughout the interviews. At some point during every provider and client interview, we asked the same three questions: How do you verify? How long does it take? What do you wish would change?

The answers were all over the place. Some relied on social media. Some required references from other providers. The providers I interviewed through that referral service only saw clients who were also members of the service. A few providers told us they had stopped verifying altogether because it was simply too difficult. Clients had a different problem: most felt it was not really their place to ask a provider to verify herself.

Among the providers who still verified, the average time they reported was 32 minutes. Unless they had met the client before, some version of that process had to happen again for every new encounter.

VAI-CHECK is where the protections already behind the V.A.I. are attached to a real encounter.

Step one: scan the QR code

The code can come from a meeting already arranged through a VAIRIFY connection protocol such as VAIRIDATE, VAIRIFY NOW, or INVITATIONS, or be created on the spot through a direct VAI-CHECK initiation. The scan connects the two V.A.I.s and brings up the verified encounter information.

Step two: review and accept the meeting

If the meeting is being created on the spot, both users review and accept the mutual-consent agreement. If it was arranged beforehand, that agreement has already been accepted and is attached to the meeting.

Step three: scan your face

The facial scan confirms that the verified user is the person standing there and taking the action.

When both users complete the required steps, the meeting becomes a verified encounter.

About five minutes to create a V.A.I.

A few seconds to verify any encounter between verified parties.

There’s no longer a need to check social media, chase down references, or go to multiple places to piece together an accurate history. You no longer have to ask awkward questions about affiliations with law enforcement or post awkward disclaimers about meetings between consenting adults.

VAI-CHECK handles all of that in a few seconds, every time.

TRUREVU

We set out to make every encounter safer—to replace uncertainty with certainty every time two people meet. What became evident during the interviews was that an accurate history was just as important.

There were so many problems with reviews that I could write an entire blog about them. But stripped down, it is pretty simple: reviews are reputation. They set expectations, and they are the most important information available in this community.

There is no system, no protection, and no standard. The reviews are owned by the platforms, not the community members who earn them. And because of that, reviews sit at the root of the majority of problems within the community.

Several providers expressed frustration with rising fees and advertising rates. When asked why they did not move to a more competitive platform, the answer was almost always the same: their business was built there and their reputation was there. If they left, they would be starting from scratch.

Essentially, they were being held hostage by the reputation they had earned.

Another provider described a client asking for a discount and threatening a bad review if she refused. Review blackmail.

Several clients told me they did not trust reviews at all. One put it bluntly: “All five stars is definitely a scam.”

During one interview, I asked a developer to go to a site, create a profile, and fill it with five-star reviews. Three hours later, it was done.

I saw the problem myself. I found a touring provider visiting my area with five-star reviews going back several months on a local directory and arranged a meeting for an interview. When I arrived, I was asked for a deposit. I knew what it was and left.

The next day, I received a call demanding $1,000. They had my home address, my wife’s name, and my children’s names, and they threatened to expose me if I did not pay.

I knew what it was. I had read about this exact scam on Reddit while researching. I thought about a person who did not know—a person who believed the threat could ruin their life.

I also thought about how sophisticated the scammers preying on the community were becoming. Using the technology available in 2022, they had gathered all of that information from a simple phone number used in a text message.

TRUREVU was designed to change that. The goal was simple: the members of the community who earned the reviews should own the reputation those reviews created.

  • Only the parties involved in a verified encounter confirmed through VAI-CHECK may submit a review.
  • Both verified parties have 48 hours from the confirmed encounter to submit their review.
  • Neither verified party can see the other’s review before submitting their own.
  • If both submit, the reviews are posted together. If only one submits, that review posts when the 48-hour window closes.

Posting both reviews at the same time prevents retaliation.

Clients rate providers on five criteria: punctuality, attitude, communication, hygiene, and overall experience.

Providers rate clients on five criteria: punctuality, attitude, communication, hygiene, and financial compliance—whether the client paid what was agreed.

TRUREVU was designed so the reputation you earn travels with you. It is attached to your V.A.I., not locked inside the platform where the encounter happened.

Your TRUREVU history travels with your V.A.I. It is attached to your profile, and your profile has its own URL. Your V.A.I. can go wherever you choose to use it, including outside platforms and social media.

Wherever your V.A.I. goes, the same verified history goes with it—carrying trust and setting expectations.

DATEGUARD

By this point, VAIRIFY could bring a verified history, a law-enforcement declaration, mutual consent, and anonymity into every encounter in seconds.

But this entire journey started in 2020 with Elizabeth Long, and her story remained my litmus test for protection. I wanted a way to protect VAIRIFY members not only before an encounter, but while it was happening.

During the interviews, several people described the systems they already used—drivers, friends, buddy systems. The idea was always the same: a trusted person knew where they were and was expected to react if something went wrong. What was missing was a reliable system for giving those people the information they would actually need.

That became DATEGUARD.

Setup

  • Create a three-digit deactivation code. This is the code that tells DATEGUARD the encounter ended safely.
  • Create a separate three-digit decoy code. It appears to deactivate DATEGUARD normally, but silently triggers the emergency response.
  • Invite the people you trust to become your guardians.
  • Each guardian accepts the invitation before being added to your guardian list. You decide who is available to protect you.

Before an encounter

  • Select the meeting you want DATEGUARD to protect.
  • Choose which guardians you want watching that encounter.
  • Take a picture of your surroundings so your guardians can see where you were when the meeting began.
  • Leave a note with anything you think they should know.
  • Set the expected length of the meeting.
  • Add a buffer period so an ordinary delay does not immediately trigger an emergency.
  • Activate DATEGUARD. Your location at activation is recorded and the timer begins.

DATEGUARD triggers in three ways

  • The timer and buffer expire without the correct deactivation code.
  • You press the panic button.
  • You enter the decoy code.

Any one of those actions starts the same emergency response.

When DATEGUARD triggers

  • Every guardian you selected is notified immediately.
  • As your guardians respond, DATEGUARD brings them into one group chat so they can communicate and coordinate with each other.
  • Your activation location and the time it was recorded are placed in the guardian chat.
  • As DATEGUARD receives updated location information from your device, those updates continue to be shared with your guardians.
  • If a current location is unavailable, the last recorded location remains visible with its timestamp.
  • DATEGUARD identifies the nearest police station and places its contact information in the same conversation.
  • The picture you took before the encounter and the note you left are provided to your guardians.

DATEGUARD was also the first time we took an idea that already existed as a standalone personal-safety app and rebuilt it specifically for this community. We looked at services like Life360, Hollie Guard, and bSafe, kept the parts that made sense, and added the protections our interviews told us were missing.

It will not be the last time we do that. In several places throughout VAIRIFY, functionality that exists elsewhere as an entire app becomes one part of the platform.

VAI PULSE

DATEGUARD effectively provided protection during an encounter. But there was another part of Elizabeth Long’s story I never forgot. After she was killed, her phone was allegedly used to continue targeting others in the community.

How could VAIRIFY have helped prevent that?

The answer was VAI PULSE.

VAI PULSE is designed to give members the information they need wherever they are, with one touch. Using their location, it searches current laws, enforcement activity, and recent news involving the community. Members can also post bulletins and share up-to-the-minute information through the community channel.

Think of it as Waze for the community.

VAI PULSE also shows how VAIRIFY will keep evolving. As technology changes, we will change with it.

Free Is a Safety Feature

The number one frustration throughout the interviews was verification itself: the time, the money, the inconvenience, handing over sensitive information to a stranger or an unfamiliar company, and often getting very little in return.

Very early, it became obvious that if verification was going to provide real protection and real value, every barrier to using it had to be removed.

Near the end of those 18 months of interviews, a provider I’ll call V suggested offering the service free for a time.

That connected the dots for me.

She was right about free. But if verification was ever going to become a real standard, it had to be free forever.

Every VAIRIFY safety feature is free to any member with a valid V.A.I.

By this point, the protections were already in place. The remaining problem was making sure everyone could actually use them.

Once the time, cost, inconvenience, and loss of anonymity are removed, the decision not to verify means something different.

It does not prove anything about the member saying no. But it gives the other verified party information they did not have before—and the choice of whether they still want to proceed.

That is why free is a safety feature.

Safety was only part of what came out of those interviews.

The other part was convenience: communication, scheduling, discovery, social features, and the everyday tools that make VAIRIFY functional, useful, and fun—bringing the entire community together in one app.

Everything you have read about so far was built to protect the community.

And all of it is free.

So the obvious question is: what could possibly be worth paying for?

That is where VAIRIFY PLUS and PREMIUM begin.

Safety had to be free. Convenience had to be worth it.

That is the next blog.
